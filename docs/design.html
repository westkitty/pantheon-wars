Pantheon Wars

Version 0.2 design: the ten pantheons. A small 4X strategy game. You are a god, and the map is the mortal world: 22×16 hexes holding 22 settlements. One human and one or two AI gods compete for worshippers over 45 turns. Every pantheon, domain, power, shrine and event is data in data/*.json. The engine is deterministic and seeded, so a seed plus a list of actions reproduces a game exactly.

A note on respect. The ten pantheons come from real traditions. Several are living religions today (Òrìṣà devotion in Yorùbá communities and the diaspora, Shintō, Hindu traditions that keep the Vedic hymns), and others have modern revivalist communities (Hellenic polytheists, Heathens, Kemetics, Celtic and Mexica revivalists). The game treats their gods as powerful, dignified and sincere, never as jokes or villains. Deities are represented only by symbols: a thunderbolt wreath, an ash tree, a winged sun, river rings, a rising field. There are no portraits, no caricatures and no reproductions of sacred icons, and invented game rites are never presented as real practice. The Fifth Sun has no sacrifice mechanics. See RESEARCH_AND_REPRESENTATION.md. Runes used as art are common, general-purpose shapes; symbols that hate groups have co-opted are deliberately avoided. Rules are original game abstractions inspired by the cited descriptions. They do not claim to describe historical or modern worship. Every mythic reference carries a citation; the full list is at the bottom.

The four Xs

Explore
The world starts hidden in cloud. Read Omens (6 Faith, once per turn) lifts the fog in radius 2 around a hex within 3 of what you already know. A Dream Vision (2 Favor, cooldown 3) reveals the nearest hidden settlement. Priests reveal the land they walk through. Each pantheon also has an explore power.
Expand
Raise a Shrine (24 Faith +8 for each shrine you already own) in a revealed settlement where you have followers, a priest, or territory. Shrines project conversion pressure 1 / 0.55 / 0.3 × strength at distance 0/1/2/3 within their reach. Ordain Priests (12 Faith; cap = 2 + 1 per shrine). They walk 2 hexes a turn and preach with pressure 0.6.
Exploit
Each follower yields 0.5 Faith per turn, plus domain bonuses for terrain and water, plus each shrine's own Faith. Winning a settlement (at least half its people follow you) grants 1 Favor and +0.25 pressure momentum there. Settlements grow every 6 turns.
Exterminate
Conversion first absorbs unaffiliated mortals, then takes rival followers. Taking one costs 1.6× the effort (scaled by the rival's resistance), and only where your pressure exceeds theirs. Eclipse (4 Favor, cooldown 2, range 3) silences a rival shrine for 3 turns and removes 1 Ward; at 0 Ward the shrine falls. Miracles strike or weigh hearts.

Resources

Faith is the everyday currency. It is earned each round and spent on shrines, priests, omens and most powers. Favor (cap 15) is the attention of the heavens. It is scarce, earned by winning settlements and through some domains, and spent on dream visions, eclipses and the great miracles. The top bar's faith meter fills toward the cost of your next shrine.

Turn structure

  1. Each god in seat order takes any number of actions, then ends its turn. An event card is drawn on turn 5 and every 5 turns after, and must be resolved first.
  2. When the last god ends its turn, the round resolves in a fixed order:
    1. priests walk (callings, roads, frost)
    2. sacred landmarks are claimed
    3. conversion (the god who acts first rotates each turn)
    4. control, Favor and pilgrim roads
    5. Faith income
    6. growth
    7. prosperity and safety
    8. cooldowns, buffs and eclipses tick down
    9. weather fronts gather, arrive and pass
    10. covenants, envoys and delayed strikes
    11. grudges cool
    12. elimination
    13. victory check (including scenario and alternative goals), then the age changes if due

Win conditions

  • Ascendancy: from turn 12, at the end of a round, at least 60% of all mortals follow you.
  • Dominion: every rival is forgotten (no followers, shrines or priests).
  • Twilight Judgment: when turn 45 ends, the highest devotion score wins. Score = followers ×1 + shrines ×3 + settlements controlled ×2 + Favor ×0.5.

Systems of version 0.2

Every rule below is also in the in-game encyclopedia (❦), generated from the same text.

Faith
Produced each turn by followers, shrines, routes and some domains. Spent on shrines, priests, omens, callings, roads and powers.
Favor
The attention of the heavens (cap 15). Earned by winning towns, keeping covenants, festivals and domains; spent on dreams, eclipses, counter-rites and miracles.
Followers and control
A town kneels to the god with the most followers once they make up half of its people. Winning a town earns 1 Favor.
Pressure
How strongly a god converts in a town each turn: nearby unshadowed shrines (stronger closer), priests standing there, control momentum and pilgrim roads, adjusted by the town's trust, hunger and fear.
The three ages
Dawn (turns 1–15): omens cost a third less, dreams recover faster. Zenith (16–30): shrines teach 15% harder and roads give +50% Faith. Twilight (31–45): eclipses cost 1 less Favor and kept covenants pay 1 more Favor.
Prosperity and safety
Every town has prosperity and safety from 0 to 10. They drift toward a level set by its land, its patron, weather, blessings and protection. A hungry town (prosperity 2 or less) does not grow and slowly abandons the god it kneels to. An afraid town (safety 2 or less) listens less to gods who cannot protect it.
Trust and memory
Towns remember who blessed them, struck them, kept or broke promises, raised or toppled shrines. Trust from −3 to +3 changes that god's pressure there by 6% a point.
Covenants
A public promise to one town: protection (your shrine still stands), plenty (prosperity reaches 6) or faithfulness (your followers do not fall). Keep it for Favor and trust; break or abandon it and pay. At most two at a time.
Counter-rites
When a rival eclipses or strikes your shrine, you may answer on your next turn for Favor: the lost Ward returns, the eclipse lifts and the attacker loses 1 Favor. A gathering storm (Storm of Susanoo) can be dispersed the same way before it breaks.
Diplomacy
Envoys propose a truce (8 turns; attacking a partner breaks it at a cost), a shared festival (both pay 4 Faith, both gain 2 Favor next turn) or a border promise (no shrines within 2 hexes of the other's shrines). Rivals remember betrayals.
Shrine callings
Each shrine may be consecrated once: Oracle (sees far, foresees weather, +1 Faith), Sanctuary (Ward, shelter, resistance) or Beacon (pressure and reach, but fragile).
Priest callings
Emissary (the baseline preacher), Seer (walks 3, sees far, preaches weakly) and Warden (walks 1, guards a shrine and steadies its town). Retrain a priest at your own shrine.
Pilgrim roads
Roads link nearby towns. Consecrate one where you have followers at both ends and a shrine at one end: it adds pressure at both ends, Faith every turn and faster priests. A rival taking an end town cuts it; a cut road lapses after a few turns.
Sacred landmarks
Wind summits, springs, groves and standing stones. A god whose priest stands alone on one at the end of a round holds it until another claims it.
Weather
Drought, storms, seasonal rains, frost and sky omens gather over a region, arrive, do their work and pass. You see weather that has arrived over land you know; coming weather only through oracles, foresight domains and omen powers.
Marks on the land
Strikes scorch, blessings flower, storms leave fallen trees, toppled shrines leave ruins (cheaper to rebuild), kept covenants leave a stone. Most fade with time.
Victory
Ascendancy: more than 60% of all mortals from turn 12. Dominion: every rival forgotten. Twilight Judgment: the highest devotion score when turn 45 ends (followers + 3 per shrine + 2 per town + half your Favor). Alternative modes add Sacred Ground and The Kept Word; scenarios set their own trials.

Event cards (19)

  • The Harvest Festival — In your largest town the mortals heap the first sheaves before your altar and sing until dawn. (Accept the offerings / Bless the fields)
  • A Comet Crosses the Sky — A pale fire hangs over the hills for three nights. Every village is watching the sky, and every village is listening. (Read it as an omen / Let them feel awe)
  • Pilgrims at the Crossroads — Travellers with staffs and dusty feet ask which road leads to the god who answers. (Guide them home / Send them onward as priests)
  • The Dry Season — Wells sink and the river shows its stones. The mortals ask whether anyone is listening. (Answer with rain (-8 Faith) / Stay silent)
  • A Rival Prophet — A wandering speaker claims another god walks the valleys. Crowds gather in the market square. (Out-preach them / Ignore them; gather offerings)
  • The Sculptor's Offer — A master mason offers a lifetime of work to whichever god will give her dreams worth carving. (Her next temple is half cost / Strengthen what stands (+1 Ward))
  • Fever in the Lowlands — Sickness moves house to house. Families light lamps and wait for a sign. (Heal them (-10 Faith) / Withdraw your gaze)
  • Storm on the Water — Fishing boats are out when the sky turns black. The harbour wives climb the headland to pray. (Calm the waters (-6 Faith) / Let the storm pass)
  • The Wandering Poet — A poet asks for one true verse about you. Whatever you give will be sung for generations. (Give a verse of glory / Give a verse of plenty)
  • An Eclipse Foretold — Star-watchers warn that the sun will be swallowed. Rivals may use the darkness. (Prepare the rites (shrines immune 3 turns) / Use the fear (+1 Favor))
  • A Market Town Swells — Traders settle where the roads meet. New faces arrive who have never heard your name. (Welcome them (+2 pop, +1 follower) / Tax the caravans (+5 Faith))
  • The Buried Altar — Farmers ploughing a hillside strike a carved stone older than any name they know. (Restore it (+1 Ward, +1 Favor) / Follow where it points)
  • Silt in the Canals — The flood has left the canals choked with mud. The fields at the far end of the water are drying out. (Call the work-gangs (-6 Faith) / Let the river choose its course)
  • The Assembly Gathers — The people come up the hill to settle disputes, race horses and hear the poets. They ask whose name opens the gathering. (Open the gathering (-4 Faith) / Keep the herds at home)
  • New Fields on the Lake — Families stake out reed fences in the shallows and heap them with lake mud. By next season there will be fields there. (Bless the new fields (-5 Faith) / Bless the fishing instead)
  • The Meadows Fall Silent — The grain will not ripen and the sheep will not lamb. The elders say a god has gone away angry and must be found. (Send out the searchers (-4 Faith) / Wait for the god to return)
  • The Smiths Open a Road — Blacksmiths cut a new road through the bush to reach the market. Traders and travellers are already on it. (Bless the road / Bless the forge)
  • Planting the Paddies — The water is let into the terraces and whole villages wade out to plant the seedlings together. (Bless the planting / Accept the first-fruit promise)
  • The Rains Are Late — The clouds pile up over the hills and do not break. Cattle stand in the dry river beds. (Release the waters (-6 Faith) / Let the cattle wait)

Trials (6 scenarios)

  • The Last Sanctuary (shrine defense, 22 turns; Hittite vs Norse vs Greek) — Two rival courts have marched their priests to your gates. Keep the sanctuary of your home town standing until the end of turn 20.
  • The Silent Valley (lost-devotion recovery, 32 turns; Irish vs Egyptian) — A long hunger has emptied your home of worshippers. Win back 40% of all mortals before the end of turn 30.
  • The Long Dark (eclipse survival, 26 turns; Japanese vs Mesopotamian vs Vedic) — The sky omens favour your rivals and their Favor overflows. Hold at least two shrines at the end of turn 24.
  • Two Rivers (contested river corridor, 32 turns; Mexica vs Yoruba) — Both of you are gods of water. Hold four waterside towns at once before the end of turn 30.
  • The Pilgrim Road (pilgrimage building, 32 turns; Yoruba vs Greek) — Open the roads. Keep three consecrated pilgrim routes active at the same time before the end of turn 30.
  • Chronicle of Three Ages (multi-age history, 45 turns; Vedic vs Mesopotamian vs Irish) — Leave a mark on every age: three shrines in the Dawn, six towns in the Zenith, and the highest devotion at Twilight.

Engine, AI and balance

  • The engine is in engine/engine.js. It has no DOM access, and all randomness comes from a mulberry32 stream stored in the state. step(state, action) is pure, and hash(state) fingerprints the result.
  • The AI has two levels, Acolyte (easy) and Hierophant (hard). Its rules are in docs/ai.md.
  • Balance: ten-pantheon final report · tuning history · v0.1 reports: before, after, 3000-game confirmation.
